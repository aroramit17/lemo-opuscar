# Stat Flash · one number, one chart, one reaction

> **Job:** inform, build authority, shock. **Voice:** optional. **Loops:** no. **Length:** 15–35 s.

## 1. Essence
A single surprising number is the hero. Everything else (a bar that outgrows its neighbours, a counter, a comparison object) exists to make that number land in 2 seconds. Not a dashboard, not a report.

## 2. Look
- The number at 300–500 px, tabular digits, in an accent colour on a quiet ground.
- One chart type per video; at most 3 data points; the highlighted one is the only coloured element.
- Source line in small type (≥ 28 px) in the lower safe band: where the number comes from.

## 3. Timing grammar
- Hook (0–2 s): the number already visible and counting. 2–5 s: what it measures, in plain words.
- 5–15 s: the comparison that gives it meaning (it's bigger than X; it grew N× since Y).
- 15–end: so what, and CTA. Hold the final number 1 s for the screenshot.

## 4. Sound
Rising ticker, a hit on the final digit, silence for 0.3 s after, then a low pad.

## 5. Native moves
- **Scale shock.** The bar or object grows until it leaves the frame. *Fits:* growth; cost; distance; time wasted.
- **Rolling digits.** Each digit spins like an odometer to its value. *Fits:* money; counts; years.
- **Split compare.** Two columns race and one is cut off. *Fits:* before/after; us vs. status quo; year over year.

## 6. Pitfalls
Numbers without a source; truncated axes that mislead; 3D charts; more than one idea. Only use data the user supplied or can source; never invent a statistic.

## 7. Variation space
Structures: *number → meaning → implication*; *guess first, then reveal*; *then vs. now*. Endings: a question for the comments, the number again, a follow CTA.
